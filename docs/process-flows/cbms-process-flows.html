Raising and permitting are different jobsAn agency holds programs.create and programs.manage; permitting is programs.approve, held by the manager that empanelled it.
Putting a batch off is asked for, not doneThe agency sends a reason in writing and the batch does not move. The manager decides, and granting it answers the request.
The exam time is the manager’sThe agency running the batch cannot set when the paper is sat.

From a conducted programme to a report

Flow 2 of 5 · page 2

What a finished batch leaves behind, and what can be drawn out of it.

Five sections, one workbookSummary, participants, attendance, trainers and monitoring. Pick any of them; up to 25 programmes go into a single file.
Filtered the way the work is organisedCategory, programme type, agency, State/UT and a date range — the same axes the scheme is allocated on.
A report never shows more than the reader may seeThe scoping is on the query, not the screen, so a narrower account cannot reach a wider report by changing the address.

Who the users are, and what each one is for

Flow 3 of 5 · page 1

Six portal tiers and one app account. Each is created by the tier above it, and each is allocated a slice of the scheme to work inside.

Super Admin owns the system, not the operationThe masters, the roles, the portal. It does not empanel, appoint managers’ coordinators, raise batches, permit them or decide profiles — it reads all of it.
The Ministry changes nothingOversight by design: every view permission, no write permission, and no way to be granted one.
A coordinator is capped, not merely ungrantedThe narrowest tier is held to a ceiling — its batches, its faculty, its material and its reports — so a mis-saved role cannot hand it the scheme.

What each tier is allocated, and what that narrows

Flow 3 of 5 · page 2

A permission says what a screen offers. The allocation says whom it may be used on — and an account allocated nothing on an axis reaches nothing on it.

An empty allocation means nothing, not everythingA scoped account with no states allocated covers no states. The opposite reading is how a narrow account quietly becomes a wide one.
Where the person sits is not what they coverAn officer posted in Delhi may be allocated fifteen States/UTs. The two are separate fields and the second is the one that governs.
The district axis is the coordinator’s aloneIt is the narrowest rung, and the only one that works at district level.

How the system reaches people

Flow 5 of 5 · page 1

Three channels out of one event: a letter by e-mail, a push to the two apps, and a list inside them that is fetched rather than remembered.

The server holds no password in the repositorySMTP host, port, user and sender are set in the portal; the password is write-only — it can be replaced but never read back.
Templates are editable without a releaseSubject and body are rows in the database with named placeholders, so wording is corrected in the portal rather than in a deployment.
Push needs one credential of yoursThe apps report an Expo push token. Delivery to Android needs a Firebase server key uploaded to the Expo project — not to this server, and not into the repository.
